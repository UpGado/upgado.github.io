While human eyes are great for many applications, they suck at providing much useful information about what we see. Humans have only three color receptors, and we pretty much only see a very small portion of the spectrum of information available in the environments we live in. Thankfully, spectroscopy comes to the rescue, providing us with immense data about a material or physical phenomena that interacts with them. This data is inherently tied to the properties of literally any material you can think of. At the Georgakoudi lab at Tufts, we work daily with spectroscopic measurements; using data-driven approaches to understand biomaterial properties or even develop tools that can diagnose diseases, including cancer.
