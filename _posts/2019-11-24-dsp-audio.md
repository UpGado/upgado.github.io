This semester at Tufts University, I have been taking this class called digital signal processing (DSP), and it was been a blast, because of the great professor and exteremely interesting material. Also, I have found out recently that this topic is of great interest to a lot of professors in Biomedical Engineering, as all professors I have talked with were happy to hear that I have taken a "signal processing class".

Today, I wanted to apply the concepts I learned in class to audio signals. I reaized that, although I listen to music 24/7, I am ignorant to the intricacies of how digital audio actually works. I had to read a bit to find out. But the main idea is that digital samples of the intensity of the  sound waves are recorded at a constant rate, represented as numbers according to one of literally hundreds of standards available, and then finally you hope that the audio was stored without any introduced artifacts or noticable loss of quality. Once this audio has been stored in a digital format, you can load itup and do all sorts of cool stuff with it! As always, let's do it in Python!
